# Image Sourcing Brief

## Goal

Digital Missions Project has strong content, but the site is still visually thin. The best improvement is to add a small set of contemporary, human, mission-aligned images rather than filling every page with generic stock photography.

## Visual Direction

Use images that feel:

- authentic
- contemporary
- warm and human
- community-oriented
- practical rather than corporate

Look for:

- real nonprofit or church-adjacent environments
- volunteers serving, organizing, planning, welcoming, mentoring
- small teams working together on laptops or around tables
- natural light
- candid expressions
- diverse people and age ranges

Avoid:

- handshakes
- call-center headsets
- glass-boardroom corporate scenes
- obvious AI or robot imagery
- overly staged worship clichés
- ultra-polished startup visuals that clash with the mission-driven tone

## Recommended Quantity

Do not try to source dozens of images. Start with 6 to 10 strong images that share a consistent style.

Suggested mix:

- 1 wide homepage hero image
- 1 supporting homepage image
- 3 real team headshots for About
- 2 to 4 service or industry images that can be reused across pages

## Placement Plan

Prioritize these areas first:

1. Homepage hero
2. Homepage supporting section
3. About page team photos
4. Service page banners or side panels
5. Industry page banners

## Notes For This Site

The site already has a clean blue palette, rounded cards, and strong typography. The images should complement that structure, not overpower it.

The site should feel:

- trustworthy
- ministry-minded
- nonprofit-aware
- practical
- modern but not trendy for its own sake

## Logo Direction

Keep the logo simple. This brand likely needs a clean wordmark and a minimal mark, not a flashy icon.

Good directions:

- `DMP` monogram in a rounded square
- subtle pathway or connection symbol
- simple wordmark with a compact emblem

Avoid:

- obvious cross icons unless intentionally desired
- techy AI symbols
- startup-style gradient mascots
- anything that feels too corporate or too church-template

## Simple Logo Tools

- Canva Logo Maker: https://www.canva.com/create/logos/
- Adobe Express AI Logo Generator: https://www.adobe.com/express/create/logo/generate

Suggested prompt:

> Create a clean, modern, mission-driven logo for Digital Missions Project. Use navy and blue tones. Avoid churches, crosses, robots, and corporate clichés. Prefer a simple geometric monogram or pathway/connection symbol. Professional, contemporary, trustworthy, human.

## Final Recommendation

1. Use real team photos on the About page.
2. Buy 5 to 8 matching images from Stocksy or Adobe Stock for the key pages.
3. Use Pexels or Unsplash only as selective filler if needed.
4. Create a simple wordmark logo in Canva or Adobe Express, then refine it if needed.
